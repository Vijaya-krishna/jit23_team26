Key Engineering Findings

1. Workload Consolidation Efficiency: EADCS dynamically concentrates task assignments onto already active servers, maintaining high CPU energy efficiency and allowing idle hosts to enter the low-power simulated sleep state.

2. Idle Power Mitigation: Traditional policies (especially Round Robin and Load Balancing) wake up and distribute tasks across all available servers. Because server idle power (e.g. 100-120W) is consumed whenever a host is powered on regardless of utilization, running servers at low load incurs severe cumulative energy penalties.

3. Latency & Deadline Preservation: EADCS applies soft overload penalties above 85-90% utilization and considers deadline slack in its multi-objective cost, preventing queue starvation while maximizing power savings.
